# Using the Monograph Management System

A guide for students, supervisors, heads of department and committee
members. Find your role below; you do not need to read the others.

---

## Signing in

Go to the address your department gave you.

- **Username** — your university ID. Students use their student ID, staff
  use their staff number. Not an email address.
- **Password** — given to you by the department administrator.

Forgotten it? Ask the administrator to reset it. The system does not send
email, so there is no "forgot password" link.

The **moon or sun** at the top right switches between light and dark. The
**bell** shows anything new. The system remembers which you chose.

---

# For students

## Where am I?

Your first screen answers that. A ring shows how far along you are, and a
track shows every stage of the journey with your current one marked.

Below it: the last thing your supervisor said, your next deadline, and — once
it is set — your defence date.

## Proposing your topic

**Monographs → Propose a topic.**

Fill in a working title, choose a research area, and describe what you want
to do. Nothing here is final: it is saved as a **draft** and you can change
every word until you submit it.

When you are ready, open your monograph and choose **Topic submitted for
approval**. Only then does the department see it. After that the topic is
locked while it is being considered.

If it comes back for changes, you can edit it again.

## Uploading your work

Open your monograph and go to **Documents → Upload a file**.

Choose what the file is — proposal, a chapter, the final monograph — and pick
the file. For a chapter, give its number.

**Uploading again does not replace anything.** It becomes version 2, and
version 1 stays where it was. Say what you changed in the note; your
supervisor sees it and it saves them re-reading everything.

Accepted: PDF, Word, PowerPoint, Excel, archives and images, up to 50 MB.

## Reading your feedback

**Feedback** shows every review you have ever been given, in order.

A review is one of three things:

- **Approved** — you may move on.
- **Revision requested** — changes are needed. Your supervisor will have said
  what.
- **Rejected** — the work cannot continue in this form.

Where your supervisor has pinned corrections to particular pages, you can
**tick each one off** as you deal with it. They can see what you have done.

## Moving forward

The buttons on your monograph are the moves you can actually make right now.
If a button you expect is missing, something is not ready yet — usually a
file that has not been uploaded, or your supervisor has not answered.

## Your defence

Once your supervisor accepts your final monograph, the head of department
sets a date and appoints a committee. It appears on your first screen and you
are notified.

After the defence you will see your result and your final grade.

# For supervisors

## What needs me today?

Your first screen shows students waiting for you, **longest wait first**. It
also shows students who have gone quiet — no progress for a month or more —
which is usually the more urgent problem.

**Reviews** in the sidebar shows the same queue plus everything you have
already answered.

## Reviewing a student's work

Open their monograph, go to **Feedback**, choose **Write a review**.

Pick your decision:

- **Approve** — the work is satisfactory and the monograph moves on.
- **Request revisions** — say what must change. This is required: a student
  cannot act on "revise this" with nothing else. The monograph goes back to
  them.
- **Reject** — the work cannot continue in this form.

Add **specific corrections** pinned to a page where you can. It is far easier
for a student to work through a numbered list than a paragraph, and you can
see which ones they have dealt with.

Your review is permanent. It is what protects you as much as them.

## Checking whether corrections were applied

Under **Documents**, a document with more than one version can be compared.
You see both versions, what the student said they changed, and the feedback
you gave between them — so you need not remember what you asked for.

## Approving individual chapters

You can approve a chapter on its own without moving the whole monograph
forward. If the student later uploads a correction to an approved document,
the approval is withdrawn automatically, because it is a different file now.

# For heads of department

## The department at a glance

Your first screen answers the questions that used to mean ringing every
supervisor:

- How many students are working, and how many have finished.
- How many submissions are sitting unanswered past your agreed response time.
- How many students have gone quiet.
- How loaded each supervisor is — **red means over their limit**.
- Where monographs spend the most time, which shows the real bottleneck
  rather than the assumed one.

## Approving a topic

Open the monograph and choose **Topic approved**. It will not let you until a
supervisor is assigned — that is deliberate, because an approved topic with
nobody guiding it goes nowhere.

If the topic is unsuitable, send it back with an explanation. The student can
then change it.

## Assigning a supervisor

On the monograph, choose **Assign supervisor**. Each person's current load is
shown next to their name, so you can spread the work.

You *can* assign someone already at their limit — sometimes there is no
choice — and the dashboard will show it in red so it is not forgotten.

## Scheduling a defence

**Defenses → Schedule a defence.** Set the date, time and place, and name the
committee. Your department's rules set the minimum committee size and the
system will not accept fewer.

Everyone involved is notified immediately.

## Recording the result

After the defence, each committee member enters their own score — nobody can
enter a score for somebody else.

Once every score is in, choose **Record the result**. The final grade is
calculated from your department's own weights (by default 40% from the
supervisor, 60% from the committee) but the result itself is whatever the
committee decided.

## Printing official forms

On any monograph, **Forms** produces:

- Topic approval form
- Supervisor assignment letter
- Defence notice
- Defence result sheet

They come out formatted with signature lines and a space for the department
stamp. A form only becomes available once the information it carries exists.

## Reports

**Reports** gives progress for the year, supervisor workload, where time is
being lost, and everyone behind schedule. **Export as CSV** produces a
spreadsheet for the faculty.

## Your department's rules

**Settings** holds everything that differs between departments: who approves
topics, how supervisors are chosen, how many students one supervisor may
carry, committee size, how the grade is weighted, and how long is too long
before the system raises the alarm.

Confirm these with your faculty before changing them. The grade weights must
add up to 100.

# For committee members

Your first screen lists the monographs you must examine.

Open one, read it, and mark **I have read it** — the head of department can
see who is prepared before the day.

After the defence, enter your score out of 100 and any comments. You may
change your own score until the result is recorded.

# For administrators

## Accounts

**People → Add a person.** Set their university ID, their name and their
role. Students also need a student ID. Email is optional and is never needed
to sign in.

Tell them their password directly — the system does not send email.

**Never delete an account.** Deactivate it instead: a graduated student's
name must still appear on their archived monograph. Deactivating stops them
signing in and changes nothing else.

## Resetting a password

**People**, then the key icon beside the person. Set a new password and tell
them.

## The archive

Finished monographs go into **Archive** automatically. Anyone signed in can
search it by title, author, supervisor or keyword. This is what future
students read instead of starting from nothing.

# Common questions

**Why can I not press a button I expect?**
Something is not ready. Usually a file has not been uploaded, or a supervisor
has not been assigned. The system says which when you try.

**I uploaded the wrong file.**
Upload the right one. It becomes a new version, and the wrong one stays in
the history rather than pretending it never happened.

**Can I delete something?**
No, and that is the point. Nothing is ever removed, which is what makes the
record trustworthy for everybody.

**Can two students write one monograph together?**
If your department allows it. Ask your head of department.

**The page is not updating.**
It usually updates by itself. On a weak connection, reload the page — nothing
is lost.

**I cannot see another student's monograph.**
You are not supposed to. Student work is private to the student, their
supervisor, the head of department and their defence committee.
